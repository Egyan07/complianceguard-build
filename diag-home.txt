FAQ
Common questions.
Which frameworks does ComplianceGuard cover?
Four, from a single collection pass: SOC 2 (43 criteria from the 2017 Trust Services Criteria), ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (all 93 Annex A controls), the HIPAA Security Rule (47 safeguards), and GDPR (38 obligations). The same OS-level evidence feeds all four, each scored by evidence coverage rather than a checkbox claim of compliance.
Will auditors accept the evidence pack?
Every evidence pack is mapped criterion-by-criterion to the AICPA Trust Services Criteria, the exact framework SOC 2 auditors work from. The PDF export is organized the way auditors expect, so there's no reformatting and no back-and-forth. A formal SOC 2 report still requires a licensed CPA firm; ComplianceGuard gets you to that engagement prepared.
How is this different from Vanta or Drata?
They collect through their cloud integrations; ComplianceGuard collects on the machines themselves. Password policies, firewall rules, disk encryption, and event logs. That evidence lives on the endpoint, not in AWS. Many teams run both: a cloud platform for SaaS integrations, ComplianceGuard for the endpoint evidence those platforms structurally can't see.
Why should I trust it with my compliance?
Don't trust claims; read the code. ComplianceGuard is source-available under BSL 1.1, so every line that touches your machine is auditable. Licensing uses offline Ed25519 public-key verification, credentials are encrypted with HKDF-derived Fernet keys, and ~1,265 tests run on every commit. Most importantly, the architecture means we never receive your evidence. It stays on your disk, under your control.
Can't I just collect evidence manually in a spreadsheet?
You can, and most first SOC 2 audits start that way. ComplianceGuard automates the collection: instead of checking firewall settings by hand, running scripts, and copying output into a spreadsheet, it reads everything in about 30 seconds and formats it the way your auditor expects.
What happens to my data?
It stays on your machine. ComplianceGuard reads from your OS and your AWS account, writes to a local SQLite database, and exports a PDF when you ask. There is no upload step and no telemetry. Evidence only moves if you explicitly enable the optional multi-machine dashboard sync.
How are my AWS credentials protected?
Credentials are encrypted at rest using a Fernet key derived via HKDF-SHA256 from your local secret key. They are decrypted in memory only at evidence-collection time and are never transmitted anywhere. The source code is available, so you can verify this yourself.
What does ComplianceGuard intentionally leave out?
No auditor marketplace. Use whichever firm you want. No 40-app integration catalog, either; the focus is evidence, not workflow chrome. And no lock-in: evidence is stored locally in standard formats, so you can export it and switch tools at any time.
